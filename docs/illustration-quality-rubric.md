# Illustration Quality Rubric: Gita Kids

The standard for judging and commissioning verse illustrations. Use it to review existing images, to decide PASS / MINOR / FAIL, and to write the `illustration_brief` field the generator draws from. It extends `docs/illustration-guidelines.md` (character sheet, style constraints). Where the two differ, this document governs review decisions; the chapter's `meta.yaml` governs which style applies.

Evidence base (2026-09-14): all 20 Ch12 images; the brief-driven images in Ch10, Ch11 and Ch13; 60 further verse images sampled from every other chapter. Calibration scores for 40 of them are in Appendix A. Three findings shape the rules below:

- Ch12 is the **style** benchmark, not a relevance benchmark. About half of Ch12 is a generic "Krishna among gopis and cows" scene (002, 006, 008, 009, 016, 020), and 011 has mosque-style minarets behind Krishna.
- The failures are consistent across chapters: a generic teacher tableau instead of the verse's content, a simile object drawn in the wrong state, text or fake script, a Buddha-like figure, a character drawn off canon, a duplicate Krishna, and modern objects or infographic devices.
- A brief can cause a defect. 13/020 asked for a "still blue witness-Self seated in serene meditation" and got a Buddha. Section 5 covers how to avoid this.

## 1. What a good image does for an 8-12 year old

**The pointing test.** Read the verse meaning aloud to a child and show the picture. The child should be able to point at one thing in the picture and say how it connects to the verse, without an adult explaining it. Examples: "the tortoise has pulled its legs in", "the lamp flame is straight", "that man is pulling the other one out of the water".

A good image:

1. **Shows the verse's own content.** It shows the named event, image, list item or behaviour. A general mood of devotion or wisdom is not enough.
2. **Makes the carrier of meaning the subject.** The object or action that carries the meaning is central and large: roughly 40% or more of the picture area, or the clear focal point.
3. **Gets the meaningful state right.** The tortoise has its limbs withdrawn, the tree is upside down, the flame is still, the fire is wrapped in smoke.
4. **Stays in the mythological world.** It uses ancient Indian settings and figures from the Mahabharata and Puranas, and never the modern analogy used in the story text.
5. **Keeps canon.** A child who has seen other pages must recognise Krishna, Arjuna, Sanjaya and Bhishma at once.
6. **Matches the chapter style** at the finish level of Ch12: a fully patterned surface, an ornate border, crisp lines and no empty filler ground.
7. **Is safe and calm enough to look at alone.** Frightening moments (Ch11 devouring mouths, death in 8.5-8.6) are shown without injury, blood or distress.

## 2. Verse-type taxonomy and depiction strategy

Classify every verse into one type before scoring or writing a brief. If a verse fits two types, use the first matching type in this order: T2, T1, T3, T4, T6, T7, T5.

| Type | Recognise it by | Depict | Failure pattern seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 Narrative / frame** | Ch1; Sanjaya's reporting verses (2.9-10, 11.9-14, 11.35, 11.50, 18.74-78); Ch11 vision verses | The literal moment: who, where, doing what. Kurukshetra verses must show the battlefield (chariots, armies, banners). Vision verses show the cosmic form and Arjuna's reaction. | 1/014 and 1/028 put the battlefield in a Pichwai garden with gopis and cows. 11/012 put a Buddha at the centre of "a thousand suns". 11/050 drew Sanjaya as a monk. |

Krishna is optional and secondary (at the side, pointing). | 2/058 tortoise walking with legs out. 15/001 an ordinary upright tree. 5/010 lotus leaf reduced to a small detail beside a dialogue. 3/038 fire with no smoke and mirror with no dust. |

For contrasts, use side-by-side panels with the same people. Krishna may watch or point. | 16/013: Krishna himself shown as the greedy thinker. 16/001: virtues as app-style icons on a tree. 14/017: sattva / rajas / tamas written as labels. 5/022: a generic group with no visible pleasure or pain. |

Use traditional emblems only. | 14/018: a vector arrow and ladder. 9/017: an earth globe with continents. |

**Two rules that apply to every type**

- **Generic tableau rule.** A Krishna-Arjuna conversation, or Krishna standing among devotees and cows, scores Relevance 2 (FAIL) for any T2, T3, T5, T6 or T7 verse. The Pichwai "Krishna among gopis" scene is the same failure in different dress. It can reach Relevance 3 only for a T6 or T5 verse whose whole content is loving devotion to Krishna (for example 12.2, 12.8, 12.20). Even then, the devotees must visibly perform the named practice: offering actions, fixing the mind, surrendering.
- **Krishna never enacts the fault.** When a verse describes greed, anger, dullness or delusion, the person showing it is an ordinary human, never Krishna, Arjuna or a sage.

## 3. Scoring scales (1-5)

Score each image on three axes. Score what is visible at normal viewing size, then zoom to check hands, arms, faces, small figures and any marks that could be letters.

### RELEVANCE: does the picture carry this verse's meaning?

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **5** | The verse's specific content (T1 moment, T2 image in the correct state, T3 items, T6 behaviour, T5 teaching scene) is the central subject. The pointing test works immediately and the setting is correct. Examples: 12/007, 9/026, 8/013, 16/004, 18/048. |
| **4** | The specific content is present and correct but shares space with filler or is not the largest element. Or one element is missing (one list item, or smoke without dust). The setting is correct. Examples: 2/070, 6/034, 8/017. |
| **3** | The right theme with a weak specific link. The key element is small, partial or ambiguous; or the right act is shown in the wrong setting (a battlefield verse in a garden); or fewer than half the list items appear; or it is a generic devotion scene for a pure-devotion verse. Examples: 1/014, 3/038, 12/002. |
| **2** | A generic tableau for a verse with specific content. Or the simile object is shown in the state that does not carry the meaning (tortoise walking, tree upright). Or a symbol so ambiguous a child would guess wrong. Examples: 2/058, 15/001, 5/022, 3/021. |
| **1** | Unrelated to the verse, contradicts it (Krishna enacting the vice), or illustrates the modern analogy. Examples: 8/006, 16/013. |

### FIDELITY: anatomy, iconography, character canon, rendering

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **5** | Every figure is anatomically correct. Canon is exact. Faces are natural for the style. No stray marks. |
| **4** | Cosmetic slips only: an extra halo on Arjuna or a devotee; Krishna's skin pale blue-grey instead of blue; a missing flute; a background glitch visible only on zoom. |
| **3** | A noticeable issue that is not a hard defect. Examples: Arjuna without a moustache; four-armed Krishna with Vishnu attributes in a verse that does not call for it (12/008, 12/015); a duplicate Krishna smaller than one-third of the main Krishna's height (1/047); a generic shaven-headed renunciate in the background (3/035); more than one Om glyph (7/008); a mask-like or uncanny face (11/002); fused hands on a secondary figure; a swastika motif. |
| **2** | One hard defect (Section 4). |
| **1** | Two or more hard defects, or a grotesque rendering. |

### STYLE: match to the chapter's folk style at Ch12 finish

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **5** | Unmistakably the chapter style (see the checklist below), with an ornate complete border, the whole surface patterned and crisp line work. Comparable to Ch12 001, 003 or 007. |
| **4** | Correct style with minor gaps: a few plain patches, a simpler border, or slight softness. |
| **3** | Recognisably the style but diluted. A large empty ground (3/038, 8/026); the wrong ground colour for the style (4/024 Warli on cream); a bright green lawn (5/018); a comic-strip panel layout; one comic device (a thought bubble). |
| **2** | Reads as a different art form or as an infographic. Arrows, icon grids, charts or maps (14/018, 16/001); flat vector clip-art; a different folk style from the chapter's. |
| **1** | Photoreal, 3D-rendered, gradient digital art, or a cartoon. |

**Style checklist (the ground and three signature features to look for)**

- **Pichwai:** dark blue, black or green ground; jewel tones with gold; strict profile or frontal figures; a gold textile border.
- **Madhubani:** double-line outlines; flat colour; every gap filled with fish, lotus or hatching; a dense border.
- **Gond:** dot-and-dash infill inside every form; nature (trees, birds, fish) as the frame; bold black outline.
- **Pattachitra:** bold black outlines with fine internal detail; cross-hatched fills; a nested multi-band border.
- **Warli:** white geometric stick figures on terracotta; circular or processional rhythm; minimal palette. A coloured Krishna figure is accepted.
- **Kalamkari:** fine pen line; natural-dye reds, ochres and indigo on cream; scrolling vines; a Tree of Life border.

## 4. Hard defects and verdict rules

### Hard defects (any one means FAIL)

- **H1. Wrong limb or anatomy count** on any clearly visible figure: three arms, a hand emerging from nowhere, two left hands, an extra leg, six fingers visible at normal size.
  - *Allowed:* the Ch11 cosmic form; Brahma's four heads; Vishnu or four-armed Krishna drawn **symmetrically and holding at least two of conch, discus, mace, lotus**; canonical multi-armed devas.
  - Four arms on Krishna *without* those attributes is H1 (2/013).
- **H2. Off-tradition or wrong religious figure or symbol.**
  - A Buddha or Buddha-like figure: cranial bump or tight curls, monk's robe over one shoulder, seated centrally in meditation or teaching pose (11/012, 13/020).
  - Also: Jain tirthankaras; Christian, Islamic, Sikh, Chinese or Japanese religious imagery; mosque domes with minarets (12/011); a deity given another deity's marks.
- **H3. Any text.** Letters, words, numbers, captions, labels, signatures, or pseudo-script in any alphabet, including Devanagari on scrolls, banners or books (7/016 caption, 14/017 labels, 9/017 fake Devanagari scroll). *Only exception:* one decorative Om glyph.
- **H4. Wrong canon on a main figure.**
  - Arjuna with blue skin, a peacock feather, a flute or a crown with feather.
  - Krishna drawn smaller than Arjuna (compare head-to-foot height, or seated equivalent), or Krishna with brown or pink human skin, or without his peacock-feather crown when he is the teacher.
  - Bhishma clean-shaven; Dhritarashtra without a blindfold.
  - Sanjaya as a shaven monk (11/050) or not recognisable as a bearded man with a topknot (18/078).
- **H5. Unrequested duplicate of a main deity or character** at one-third or more of the main figure's height, so a child cannot tell which is Krishna (12/017 blue haloed child, 17/010 second crowned blue figure, 11/043 two Vishnus). A smaller background vignette is Fidelity 3 instead.
- **H6. Krishna, Arjuna or a sage enacting** the fault the verse warns against (16/013).
- **H7. Unsafe for children:** blood, wounds, severed or crushed heads, weapons piercing bodies, corpses shown in pain, nudity, or mockery of any caste, gender or group. Ch11 devouring verses pass only when shown as warriors streaming into light or into a stylised mouth, with no injury.
- **H8. Modern objects or analogy:** a globe or map, clocks, gym weights (4/037), modern clothes or buildings, sports equipment, school settings, screens.
- **H9. Unrelated to the verse** (Relevance 1).

### Verdict

| Verdict | Rule | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **FAIL** | Any hard defect, OR Relevance ≤ 2, OR Style ≤ 2, OR Fidelity ≤ 2 | Regenerate. Write or fix the `illustration_brief` first. |
| **MINOR** | No hard defect, all three scores ≥ 3, and at least one score = 3 | Keep for now; log the issue. Regenerate only in a later polish pass or if a retouch cannot fix it. |
| **PASS** | No hard defect and all three scores ≥ 4 | Keep. |

### Tie-breakers for consistent calls

- **When unsure whether a mark is text,** zoom to 200%. If it has letter-like strokes arranged in a line, it is H3.
- **When unsure whether a figure is Buddha-like,** check for the cranial bump or tight curls, the robe over one shoulder, and the central seated pose. Two of the three make it H2.
- **Hindu rishis** have matted hair in a bun (jata), a beard, rudraksha beads or a tilak. A shaven head alone, on a background figure, is Fidelity 3.
- **Judge the verse, not the story.** Read the meaning and transliteration. Never raise Relevance because the story text mentions something in the picture.
- **Score the benchmark by the same rules.** Ch12 images can FAIL on relevance or fidelity while remaining the style reference.
- **Covers** are emblematic by design. Apply Fidelity, Style and H1-H8, and score Relevance against the chapter summary, not a single verse.
- **Record every review as one line:** `ch/verse | type T# | R F S | verdict | defects (H#) | one-line fix`.

## 5. Writing an `illustration_brief`

The generator places the brief in the prompt as "Scene to depict (draw this literally and specifically)", and passes the verse meaning only as context. Two things follow from how `getRelevantCharacters` works:

- **Krishna is always included** when he is the speaker, and **Arjuna is included whenever the meaning says "Partha", "Kaunteya" and similar names.** If either should be small or absent, the brief must say so.
- **The model draws nouns.** Every word in the brief should name something visible.

### Template (2-4 sentences, about 50-100 words)

1. **Sentence 1, the carrier of meaning.** The key object, action or scene, in the exact state that carries the verse, stated as the largest or central element.
2. **Sentence 2, characters.** Who else appears, with canon details for any main character, and what each is doing. Give Krishna's size and position.
3. **Sentence 3, setting and composition.** Place (Kurukshetra, forest hermitage, village courtyard, cosmic sky), foreground and background, and zones for lists or contrasts.
4. **Sentence 4, exclusions.** "Exclude: ..." Always end with "no text, letters or script". Add the confusions this verse type invites.

### Brief-writing rules

- **Replace abstract nouns with visible behaviour.** "Equanimity" becomes "the same calm face turned toward the friend and the attacker".
- **Never write "a meditating figure" unqualified.** Write "a Hindu rishi with a matted hair bun, white beard and rudraksha beads", or use an aniconic symbol (flame in a lotus, ring of light).
- **Avoid words that invite writing.** Vedas, scriptures, scroll, book, sign, map, label. Write "sages chanting with open mouths" instead of "the Vedas".
- **Name each list item with its iconography.** For example "Vasuki, a great many-hooded serpent king".
- **Do not ask for a second Krishna.** If the verse contrasts two forms, say "one Krishna only" and describe the second form as a glow or silhouette behind him (as in 11/003).
- **Put Kurukshetra verses on the battlefield**, and exclude cows, gopis and lotus ponds.
- **State the canon line whenever Krishna and Arjuna share the frame.** "Krishna largest, blue skin, yellow silk, peacock-feather crown, halo; Arjuna brown skin, black moustache, terracotta armour, plain headband, holding Gandiva."

### Worked examples

**E1 · T2 built-in image · 2.58 (tortoise withdrawing limbs; Gond)**
> A large patterned tortoise at the centre of a riverbank, shown with its head and all four legs pulled completely inside its shell, while ripe fruit, a flower and a darting fish lie untouched close around it. Krishna (blue skin, yellow silk, peacock-feather crown, halo) sits small at the left under a tree, pointing to the tortoise. Trees, birds and fish frame the scene. Exclude: tortoise walking or with legs or head out; Arjuna; no text, letters or script.

**E2 · T1 narrative · 1.14 (Krishna and Arjuna blow their conches; Pichwai)**
> On the Kurukshetra battlefield, Krishna (largest figure, blue skin, yellow silk, peacock-feather crown, halo) stands as charioteer and Arjuna (brown skin, black moustache, terracotta armour, plain headband, Gandiva on his shoulder) stands beside him; both raise white conch shells to their lips. Their golden chariot with four white horses fills the centre, and sound spreads as gold wave patterns over rows of Pandava soldiers and banners. Exclude: gardens, cows, gopis, lotus ponds; no text, letters or script.

**E3 · T3 list · 10.27 (Uchchaihshravas, Airavata, the king)**
> Three zones. Left: Uchchaihshravas, a snow-white horse, rising from a churning ocean of milk. Centre: Airavata, a white elephant with four tusks, carrying Indra (gold crown, thunderbolt). Right: a just king on a throne, sheltering his people with an open hand. Krishna (blue skin, yellow silk, peacock-feather crown, halo) stands small between the zones, gesturing to all three. Exclude: brown or ordinary horses; Arjuna; no text, letters or script.

**E4 · T5 abstract, via Puranic scene · 8.6 (the last thought decides what one becomes)**
> In a forest hermitage, the old royal hermit Bharata (white beard, matted hair bun, simple ochre cloth) lies peacefully on a grass mat, his gaze and a thin golden thread of light going from his heart to the spotted fawn he raised, which rests its head beside him. Above, in a small cloud medallion, the same fawn leaps in a forest, showing where his thought carries him. Krishna appears only as a small haloed figure in the top corner. Exclude: distress, tears or illness; Arjuna; no text, letters or script.

**E5 · T6 behaviour · 17.10 (tamasic food)**
> In a dim village courtyard, a heavy-lidded man slumps half-asleep beside a leaf-plate of stale leftovers: dried rotis, cold spilled rice, a cracked clay pot, a few small flies. A clean, bright kitchen hearth stands unused behind him. Krishna (blue skin, yellow silk, peacock-feather crown, halo) stands at the right edge, pointing; one Krishna only. Exclude: any second blue-skinned or crowned figure; rot shown in disgusting detail; no text, letters or script.

**E6 · T4 dialogue · 12.1 (Arjuna asks: worship of the form or the formless?)**
> In the chariot on Kurukshetra, Arjuna (brown skin, black moustache, terracotta armour, plain headband, Gandiva across his knees) turns to Krishna (larger, blue skin, yellow silk, peacock-feather crown, halo), both palms open in a questioning gesture. In the upper left, a small vignette shows devotees offering flowers to a visible form of Krishna. In the upper right, rishis sit before a formless field of light. Exclude: a Buddha or any robed monk; no text, letters or script.

**E7 · T2 built-in image · 15.1 (the upside-down Ashvattha)**
> A huge peepal tree growing upside down: its roots spread across the top of the picture into a starry heavenly sky, its trunk hangs down through the centre, and its branches with heart-shaped leaves spread along the bottom over villages, animals and people. Small sages chanting with open mouths sit among the leaves. Krishna (blue skin, yellow silk, peacock-feather crown, halo) stands at the left, pointing up to the roots. Exclude: an upright tree with roots at the bottom; writing on leaves; no text, letters or script.

**Correction example (13/020, which produced a Buddha)**
- *Before:* "a still blue witness-Self seated in serene meditation, watching, untouched."
- *After:* "On the right, the witness-Self shown as a still golden flame inside an open lotus on a dark ground, untouched by the swirl beside it. Exclude: any seated meditating monk, Buddha, shaven head or robe over one shoulder."
